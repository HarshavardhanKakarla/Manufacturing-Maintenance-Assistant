# Fictional Conveyor System — Maintenance Reference

This fictional document is for demonstrating a document-grounded assistant. It
does not describe real equipment and must not be used to service real machinery.
Always follow the manufacturer's manual and the facility's approved procedures.

## Safety

- Only trained and authorized personnel may inspect or maintain the conveyor.
- Before inspection, isolate all energy sources and follow the facility's
  lockout/tagout procedure.
- Never remove or bypass a guard, safety interlock, or emergency stop to keep
  the conveyor running.
- If a hazardous condition is present or the correct procedure is unclear, stop
  work and contact the maintenance supervisor.

## Conveyor motor overheating

If the motor temperature warning appears, stop the conveyor using the approved
operating procedure and notify authorized maintenance personnel. After the
equipment has been made safe under the facility's lockout/tagout procedure, the
manual lists these inspection points:

1. Check whether the motor ventilation openings are blocked by dust or debris.
2. Check the maintenance log for repeated overload warnings.
3. Check that the conveyor belt is not visibly obstructed or overloaded.
4. Record the motor warning and observed conditions for the maintenance team.

This reference does not provide temperature limits, electrical test procedures,
or instructions for restarting the equipment. Consult the manufacturer's
technical manual and a qualified technician for those steps.

## Unusual belt noise

If unusual noise is reported, stop and report the condition to authorized
maintenance personnel. Once the equipment is made safe following the approved
lockout/tagout procedure, the listed visual checks are:

- Look for visible debris near the belt path.
- Record when the noise occurs and whether the belt appears to track off-center.

Do not reach into the conveyor or remove guards. This reference does not specify
adjustment or repair procedures.

## Documentation

Record the equipment identifier, date and time, warning or symptom, operating
conditions reported by the operator, and any observations. Do not record a
diagnosis unless it has been confirmed by authorized maintenance personnel.
